🩺 Air Quality & Health Guide

US EPA Air Quality Index standards, category meanings, and recommended precautions

0 – 50

Good

Air quality is good. Enjoy outdoor activities.

General Public: Ideal air quality for all outdoor sports, running, and cycling.

Sensitive Groups: No restrictions. Safe for children, elderly, and individuals with respiratory conditions.

Ventilation: Great time to open windows and air out indoor spaces.

51 – 100

Moderate

Air quality is acceptable. Unusually sensitive people should consider reducing prolonged outdoor exertion.

General Public: Normal outdoor activity is generally safe.

Sensitive Groups: Individuals with severe asthma should monitor for mild symptoms during prolonged exertion.

Ventilation: Windows can remain open under moderate conditions.

101 – 150

Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups

Sensitive groups should reduce prolonged outdoor exertion.

General Public: Most adults will not experience immediate acute effects.

Sensitive Groups: Children, the elderly, and people with lung/heart disease should limit strenuous outdoor play or exercise.

Ventilation: Keep windows closed near heavy traffic corridors.

151 – 200

Unhealthy

Everyone should reduce prolonged outdoor exertion. Consider a mask outdoors.

General Public: Noticeable throat irritation or coughing may occur during intense exertion. Wear a well-fitted N95 mask outdoors.

Sensitive Groups: Avoid prolonged outdoor activities completely. Stay indoors in air-filtered spaces.

Ventilation: Keep windows closed; run HEPA air purifiers if available.

201 – 300

Very Unhealthy

Avoid outdoor exertion. Wear an N95 mask if you must go outside.

General Public: Significant risk of respiratory symptoms for the entire population. Move workouts indoors.

Sensitive Groups: Remain indoors. Keep quick-relief medications readily accessible.

Ventilation: Seal window gaps; avoid burning incense or candles indoors.

301+

Hazardous

Hazardous. Stay indoors with windows closed. Avoid all outdoor activity.

General Public: Health warning of emergency conditions. Everyone is likely to be affected.

Sensitive Groups: Severe risk of exacerbation of heart or lung diseases. Minimize any physical exertion.

Ventilation: Keep all doors/windows sealed. Use HEPA filtration continuously.

👤 Tailored Health Alert Thresholds

Delhi AQI Sentinel lets you select a profile on the Live Map to receive proactive warnings along your travel routes:

Asthma & Respiratory

Alert at AQI 100

Fine particles (PM2.5) trigger airway inflammation quickly. Alerts trigger whenever route points enter the Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups band.

Children

Alert at AQI 100

Children breathe faster relative to body mass and their lungs are developing. Alert warns against prolonged sports when AQI crosses 100.

Elderly

Alert at AQI 150

Cardiovascular strain rises markedly in older adults above AQI 150. Proactively alerts before prolonged exposure occurs.

Healthy Adult

Alert at AQI 250

High exertion in Very Unhealthy air poses systemic oxidative stress. Warns when exposure along a route approaches hazardous levels.

ℹ️ About Delhi AQI Sentinel

Real-time air quality mapping and pollution-minimized route optimization

🗺️

Continuous Spatial Interpolation (IDW)

Most air quality services display static sensor readings at a handful of government monitoring stations. If you live 5 km away from the nearest station, that number is only a rough guess. Delhi AQI Sentinel integrates all continuous ambient stations across Delhi NCR in real time and calculates an Inverse Distance Weighting (IDW) spatial estimate. This generates a smooth, hyper-local continuous air quality field so you can inspect estimated pollution at any street, park, or landmark.

🧭

Pollution-Aware Route Intelligence

Traditional GPS apps navigate strictly for travel duration or distance. However, in heavily polluted metropolitan environments, taking a major congested highway can expose you to significantly higher pollution than a slightly longer, greener bypass. Delhi AQI Sentinel computes the true total exposure along every alternative route:

Total Exposure = Σ (AQI at sampled point × Segment duration in minutes) × Breathing Exertion Factor

Because pedestrians and cyclists inhale substantially more air per minute than motorists inside a vehicle cabin, physical exertion multipliers (2.5× for Walking, 2.2× for Cycling, 1.0× for Driving) ensure route comparisons reflect genuine physiological lung burden.

🔒

Privacy-First & High-Performance Proxy

The application runs with a dedicated local FastAPI proxy backend that caches station readings server-side for 10 minutes and applies Token Bucket rate-limiting (1 req/sec per IP) to guard upstream quotas. No personal location data, search history, or saved places ever leave your browser, providing lightning-fast navigation with complete privacy.
